## Who held the post, in what order, 2005-2013?

### Takeaway
Best-supported order: (1) Salim Salih Mahdi al-Maslamawi (سالم صالح مهدي المسلماوي), SCIRI/ISCI, elected after the 2005 council vote, until 2009; (2) Salman Hassan al-Zarkani (سلمان حسن الزركاني), 2009 to March 2011, Sadrist-linked or independent; (3) Muhammad Ali al-Masoudi (محمد علي المسعودي), elected 18 March 2011, apparently in office at the April 2013 election. No source in hand gives exact start/end dates for the first two or confirms a vacancy.

### Cited Findings
- 2005: council elected "Salem al-Saleh Meslmawe" governor; party SCIRI (ISCI) per Wikipedia-derived page (SINGLE-SOURCE; summary only, page not opened) — [2005 Babil governorate council election](https://en.wikipedia.com/wiki/2005_Babil_governorate_council_election)
- The 2009 Babil election infobox lists Salim al-Mesalmaoui (ISCI) as the prior governor and Salman Hassan al-Zarkani (Sadrist Movement) as the post-election governor — [2009 Babil governorate election](https://www.doc.surf/wiki/2009-babil-governorate-election) (Wikipedia mirror, aggregator)
- Name variants: "Babil Governor Salam Salih Mahdi al-Muslimawi" appears in a 2008 report on a late-2007 sit-in, so he was governor in late 2007 (SINGLE-SOURCE, from search summary of a refworld/UNHCR PDF) — [refworld PDF](https://webarchive.archive.unhcr.org/20230529230923if_/https://www.refworld.org/pdfid/4b6fe25e0.pdf). ### Inferences
- Order Maslamawi, then Zarkani, then Masoudi is consistent across sources; the user's earlier ordering (Maslamawi, Masoudi, Zarkani) is wrong: Zarkani preceded Masoudi.
- Since the 2009 infobox shows Maslamawi as the pre-2009 governor and Zarkani was named after the January 2009 election, Maslamawi most likely served roughly 2005 to spring 2009.
- Masoudi was probably governor until the post-April-2013 council chose Sadiq Madlool, but no source confirms the handover date.

### Gaps

- Exact date Zarkani was chosen in 2009 (Musings says the council vote came by about April 2009; Muthanna was the last council to vote, on 30 April 2009 — [Musings, all new provincial councils](https://musingsoniraq.blogspot.com/2009/05/all-of-iraqs-new-provincial-councils.html), page not opened).
- Masoudi's party (only "leaning ISCI/independent of State of Law") and the date he left.

## Was there any vacancy, acting governor, or dismissal 2005-2013?

### Takeaway
No source found confirms a vacancy of about a year before Zarkani. The only documented gap is March 2011 (Zarkani resigned under Maliki pressure; Masoudi elected 18 March 2011 per Wikipedia list), which is days to weeks, not a year.

### Cited Findings
- Iraq Business News: Babel was one of the most contested governorates in agreeing a governor after the January 2009 elections (long deadlock), which may be the source of the "vacancy" claim — [Complex Alliances in Babel](https://www.iraq-businessnews.com/?p=20691) (search summary; two different pieces under one title may exist)
- Zarkani's exit was a forced resignation, not a formal dismissal — [Complex Alliances in Babel](https://www.iraq-businessnews.com/2011/03/28/complex-alliances-in-babel/)

### Inferences
- A year-long vacancy would most plausibly be the 2009 post-election deadlock (Jan to about spring 2009) with Maslamawi possibly continuing in caretaker capacity; this is speculation.

### Gaps
- No acting governor name found for any period 2005-2013; the "year vacant" source was not located.

## Who was in office after the 2009 provincial elections?

### Takeaway
Salman Hassan al-Zarkani (سلمان حسن الزركاني), listed as Sadrist/Independent Trend of the Noble Ones (or independent), took over from Maslamawi in 2009 and served until his March 2011 resignation.

### Cited Findings
- 2009 Babil election held 31 January 2009; State of Law 8 seats and Martyr al-Mihrab list 5 of 30 per Wikipedia table (mirrors disagree on details) — [2009 Babil governorate election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Babil_governorate_election)
- Governor Zarkani, party conflict as above — [Musings, July 2009](https://musingsoniraq.blogspot.com/2009/07), [Cable 09BAGHDAD1283](https://wikileaks.jcvignoli.com/cable_09BAGHDAD1283?hl=PolOff)

### Inferences
- Maslamawi (ISCI) lost the post because ISCI's vote fell in 2009 and State of Law and Sadrists held the balance.
